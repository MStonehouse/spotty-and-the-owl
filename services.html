Screen printing

Screen printing is the classic method behind most team shirts and company tees, and still the best value for larger orders. Each colour in your design gets its own fine mesh screen, and ink is pressed through it onto the garment one layer at a time. The result is a bold, opaque print with crisp edges that holds up wash after wash. Because the setup is done once, the cost per garment drops as your order grows.

Best suited for

  • Business and company apparel
  • School and team shirts
  • Event merchandise
  • Larger quantity orders
  • Bold logos and graphic designs

Each colour usually needs its own screen, so the number of colours in your design affects the setup cost.

Embroidery

Embroidery stitches your logo directly into the fabric with thousands of tightly packed threads, for a raised, textured finish that looks polished and lasts as long as the garment. It's the professional choice for anything worn at work, from polos and jackets to caps and uniforms.

Best suited for

  • Company polos
  • Workwear
  • Jackets and outerwear
  • Hats and caps
  • Uniforms and corporate apparel

Your artwork is first converted into a stitch file (digitizing), which tells the machine every stitch. A one-time digitizing charge may apply, and the file is kept for reorders.

Heat-transfer vinyl

Heat-transfer vinyl is cut to the exact shape of your lettering, then pressed onto the garment with heat. The edges come out clean and sharp, and every piece can be different, which makes vinyl the go-to for player names, numbers and one-off personalization.

Best suited for

  • Sports team names and numbers
  • Individual personalization
  • Business lettering
  • Simple logos and graphics

Different vinyl types suit different fabrics. We'll match the vinyl to your garment.

DTF printing

Direct-to-Film printing puts your full-colour design onto a special film, which is then heat-pressed onto the garment. It reproduces gradients, fine detail and small text that screens can't, with no screens to set up, so it's ideal when a design has many colours but the run is small.

Best suited for

  • Multi-colour artwork
  • Detailed logos
  • Small and medium production runs
  • Designs that are not practical for screen printing

Fabric, garment construction and care instructions all play a part. We'll confirm your garment is a good match before printing.

DTG printing

Direct-to-Garment printing works like an inkjet printer for apparel: ink is printed straight into the fabric, so even photographic artwork comes out detailed and soft to the touch. With no screens to make, it suits small runs and one-offs.

Best suited for

  • Detailed artwork
  • Photographic-style designs
  • Small production runs
  • Cotton garments and compatible fabrics

DTG performs best on cotton. Garment colour and pretreatment can affect the result, and we'll advise on both.

Not sure which method is right?

Each method has its strengths. Send us your artwork, the garment you have in mind and a rough quantity, and we'll recommend the best fit for your project.
